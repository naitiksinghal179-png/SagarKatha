# Problem statement

## The short version

India's fishers carry a detailed, practical understanding of the sea – which fish arrive with which wind, how the water changes colour before a catch, what a flat calm before the monsoon means, which tales keep a crew careful. Almost all of it is **oral**, tied to **local dialects**, and held by **older people**. Very little of it is written down in a form that younger fishers, researchers and planners can search, hear or map.

## Why it matters

- **Scale.** The CMFRI *Marine Fisheries Census 2016* counts **3,477 marine fishing villages**, **8,93,258 fisher families**, about **37.7 lakh (3.77 million) fisherfolk** and **1,363 landing centres** along India's coast.
- **A moving sea.** Fishers describe fish arriving later or farther out, shorelines eroding and weather signs becoming less reliable. Whether or not each individual observation is right, they are early, local, first-hand signals – exactly what scientific monitoring struggles to collect at village scale.
- **A fragile carrier.** Knowledge that lives only in speech disappears when the speaker does, and when a dialect stops being spoken at home.
- **Safety.** Many safety rules are told as stories (for example the Bonbibi tales of the Sundarbans: *go in groups, take only what you need*). Losing the story loses the rule.

## Who is affected

| Group | Problem today |
|---|---|
| Fishers and elders | Their knowledge is rarely recorded, credited or used; apps and forms are not built for their languages or for weak networks. |
| Younger coastal people | Little access to elders' knowledge and tales in an engaging form. |
| Researchers and fisheries planners | Hard to find ground-level, geo-tagged, dated observations in local languages. |
| Language and culture communities | Coastal dialect vocabulary is undocumented and varies village to village. |

## Why a simple "upload your story" site is not enough

1. **Consent and control.** Community knowledge must not be harvested. People need to say yes, choose what is shared, and withdraw.
2. **Location is sensitive.** A fishing ground is livelihood information. Exact pins can be misused.
3. **Language.** The 22 scheduled languages plus unscheduled coastal dialects must all be first-class.
4. **Trust.** An archive that mixes real recordings with demo content, or presents animal sightings as live tracking, would damage trust.

## What we do not know yet

This repository is a working prototype, not a validated product. Before relying on it we would need to test with real fishing communities:

- Do fishers want to record in this form (phone microphone in a browser), or would a WhatsApp / voice-call flow work better? (SagarKatha began as a WhatsApp bot.)
- Which topics and which privacy settings do they actually choose?
- How accurate are machine transcripts in coastal dialects? (Expect errors; the app always lets the contributor correct the text.)
- Who should moderate, in which languages?
